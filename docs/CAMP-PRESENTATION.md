# Army camp occupants

The village now creates one ambient actor for each prepared troop, rather than showing a fixed eight-character sequence or treating housing spaces as individual soldiers. All seven troop types use their movement artwork. Balloons float above roofs and leave a ground shadow; Goblins and Wall Breakers use their specialist walk and planted idle frames.

Troops are distributed across every completed camp according to housing load and each camp’s actual capacity. A constructing camp has no occupants until it finishes. Upgrading an existing camp continues to house troops, matching its contribution to the model's capacity. Army edits, replenishment, imports, camp moves and layout changes rebuild the assignment, while stable troop identifiers allow existing sprite objects to be reused. Emptying the army removes its actors and shadows. Battle entry hides them; returning home restores the current army.

Ground routes use clear tile centers around the actual center of each 4×4 camp. The footprint and proportional artwork correction are documented in [NATIVE-GRID.md](NATIVE-GRID.md). Every walking segment connects adjacent free tiles, so its foot position stays outside building and wall footprints. A tightly surrounded camp uses its nearest clear tile. Actors pause between short walking segments and face their travel direction. Their private home-village animation clock stops during battles and with reduced motion. Enabling reduced motion freezes current positions rather than moving everyone to an unrelated idle position.

The renderer retains a 660-actor stress limit derived from the legacy catalog: four level-eight camps plus the prototype base allowance. Normal TH8 progression stops at camp level six; neither this legacy limit nor the prototype capacity formula describes native army capacity. The visible actors of over-capacity imported saves are bounded to this rendering maximum, with a round-robin selection that retains all present troop kinds. These ambient actors do not change combat, army quantities or saved progression. Patrol paths, pacing and visual scale are local presentation choices; this is not a claim of exact live-game camp behavior.

Six unit cases cover exact individual counts, all four camps, capacity-weighted housing, construction, the maximum roster and oversized imports, stable identity after a move, and sampled full walking segments beside obstructions and map edges. Four browser scenarios exercise real army editing, airborne artwork/shadows, object removal, reduced motion, battle transitions, a 660-actor roster, camp moves and phone rendering. `node scripts/performance-check.mjs --camps` adds a maximum-camp rendering sample to the normal village and battle benchmark.

September 11 local headless Chromium sample at 1440 × 960 initially measured 49 FPS for the 660-actor stress case. The [sprite rendering changes](SPRITE-RENDERING.md) raise that sample to 60 FPS with a 16.7 ms 95th-percentile frame, retaining every actor and visible artwork pixel. A controlled comparison of the same village measured 50 FPS with the original triangle-strip topology and 60 FPS with independent triangles. The benchmark identifies the actual renderer as ANGLE SwiftShader. Physical-device measurements remain open; these local samples are not a general 60-FPS certification.

## Native progression

[Camp progression](CAMP-PROGRESSION.md) now supplies explicit capacity, health, cost, duration and Town Hall count tables. The model has no prototype base housing allowance. A new TH2 village has one level-two camp and 30 spaces. Prepared troops in existing saves are retained even if they exceed the corrected limit; the Army drawer explains how to make room. Actor distribution uses each camp's actual capacity, while the separate 660-actor safety limit preserves the old visible roster.

Native per-level camp artwork remains unfinished; the current tents and perimeter wall are original stylized assets. The open fire-pit and cooking-support progression is being developed separately from these verified gameplay values.
